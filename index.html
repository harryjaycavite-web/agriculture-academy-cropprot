Plant Pathology

🔺 The Plant Disease Triangle

  • Susceptible Host — a vulnerable cultivar, wounded tissue, or stressed plant provides the infection site.
  • Virulent Pathogen — fungi, bacteria, viruses, nematodes, and phytoplasmas each cause characteristic symptoms.
  • Conducive Environment — temperature, leaf wetness, humidity, and inoculum load determine epidemic development.

Entomology

🐞 Insect Biology & Pest Identification

  • Complete Metamorphosis — egg → larva → pupa → adult; larvae and adults often have different food habits.
  • Incomplete Metamorphosis — egg → nymph → adult; nymphs resemble miniature adults and share habitat/food.
  • Mouthparts & Damage — chewing insects remove tissue; piercing-sucking insects cause chlorosis, curl, and virus transmission.

Weed Science

🌿 Weed Competition & Management

  • Critical Period of Weed Control — the crop stage when weed removal prevents unacceptable yield loss.
  • Herbicide Selectivity — selective herbicides suppress target weeds while sparing the crop; non-selective herbicides injure most plants.
  • Integrated Weed Management — combines prevention, mechanical control, cultural practices, and judicious herbicide use.

Vertebrate & Other Non-Insect Pests

🐀 Beyond Insect Pests

  • Vertebrate Pests — rodents, birds, bats, and wild pigs damage crops through feeding, burrowing, and contamination.
  • Mites — arachnids with four pairs of legs as adults; common symptoms include stippling, bronzing, and webbing.
  • Plant-Parasitic Nematodes — microscopic roundworms that damage roots, reducing water and nutrient uptake.

Pesticide Toxicology & Application

🧴 Safe & Effective Pesticide Use

  • Toxicity & LD₅₀ — the dose that kills 50% of test animals; lower LD₅₀ means higher acute toxicity.
  • Formulation & Dilution — correct mixing maintains label-recommended dose, safety, and target efficacy.
  • Application Safety — calibrated equipment, PPE, label compliance, and observing pre-harvest intervals are essential.

Domain 6 of 6

Integrated Pest Management (IPM)

📊 Economic Decision-Making in IPM

  • Economic Injury Level (EIL) — the lowest pest density at which damage equals the cost of control.
  • Economic Threshold (ET) — pest density at which action should be taken to prevent the EIL from being reached.
  • IPM Tactics — prevention, monitoring, biological/cultural/physical control, and selective chemical use as a last resort.
